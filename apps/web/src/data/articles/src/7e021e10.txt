What is AGI. Four definitions
One term, four definitions, and they don't match:
OpenAI, the charter: a highly autonomous system that outperforms humans at most economically valuable work. Definition through economy.
2.     Chollet, author of ARC: intelligence is the efficiency of skill acquisition. Not "what it can do", but "how fast it learns something new with little experience". Definition through learning.
3.      DeepMind, "Levels of AGI": a ladder of levels, from "emerging" to "superhuman", separately by depth and by breadth. Definition through a scale.
4.      Helen Toner, ex-board of OpenAI: the term is "almost useless", because everyone puts his own meaning inside. Definition through refusing to define.
When OpenAI showed the Astra numbers on ARC, CfB wrote: the test was about necessity, but they sold it as sufficiency. Meaning: fail ARC and you are surely not AGI. Pass ARC and it doesn't mean AGI. These are two different statements, and the market mixes them on purpose. 
More precisely, the marketing people mix them for you.
What the metrics in the table mean
ARC-AGI-3 is games. Interactive, no instructions, you have to discover the rules yourself. The key parameter here: adaptation.
Three sets: 25 public demo games (not counted in the score), 55 semi-private (for models via API), 55 fully private (Kaggle, offline).
The score is counted per level: how many actions a human spent, how many the agent spent, the ratio squared, weighted by level, averaged over games. The result is in percent.
Human is 100%. This is the reference point.
Now the rows.
Gemini 3.1 Pro at launch: 0.37%.
GPT 5.4 at launch: 0.26%.
Opus 4.6 Max at launch: 0.25%.
Grok-4.20: 0.00%.
This is the standard harness, no add-ons. This is how the frontier looked in summer: zero with a tail.
GPT-6 Astra, September 3, verified by ARC Prize: 62.7% in the standard harness, cost of the run 26,098 dollars (you feel it already?). With the provider adapter, meaning continuous memory between moves: 99.9% for 18,817 dollars.
Chollet called it a "step-function change" and wrote about 360 dollars per game.
ARC Prize, at the same time, wrote separately: we are not claiming this is AGI.
Kaggle track: 50 dollars compute budget, offline, open source. Same test, different weight category.

What is the difference of approaches
An LLM learns on everything people ever wrote and predicts the next token. More data and more GPUs, the better.
Aigarth doesn't read text. It grows a network. From noise, by selection, on the compute of miners. Not billions of neurons, but 64, and CfB is preparing the move to 128.
Last week he confirmed the mechanism: the network as a graph, degenerate and near-degenerate topologies get thrown out, the task is solved with fewer neurons, the scaling goes sublinear "because of graph isomorphism".
Astra talks. Aigarth grows.
With Astra you can't verify that it understood. Aigarth is verified by the score: 0 errors means the best model for the task.
