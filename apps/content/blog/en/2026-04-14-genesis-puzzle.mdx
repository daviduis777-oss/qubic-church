## Thirteen findings

The 65-byte Genesis public key simultaneously satisfies two independent constraints:

1. It is a valid point on the secp256k1 curve.
2. It parses as a coherent four-instruction Bitcoin Script: `PUSHBYTES_4`, `OP_PUBKEY`, `OP_5`, `PUSHBYTES_72`.

The dual-purpose encoding means the bytes were deliberately chosen to satisfy both the curve equation and the Script opcode structure simultaneously. That is a designed construction, not a random pubkey.

## Matrix resonance

The script contains `PUSHBYTES_72` (opcode 0x48 = 72). Looking up position `matrix[72][72]` in the Anna Matrix yields `-27`. Earlier text read this as the constant that selects 10 early Bitcoin addresses containing 500 BTC; that selection was an off-by-one index error and was not confirmed.

When the 65 pubkey bytes are encoded as a ternary state and iterated through the Anna Matrix, the system converges to a period-4 cycle at step 11 with sum signature `(-42, 50, 38, -56)`. This observation motivated follow-up work on the seven-position signature, which was later not confirmed.

## On-chain trinity

Three Qubic addresses (POCC, HASV, PXMARAS) issued the community tokens GENESIS, EXODUS and AIGARTH, with initial supplies of 676 billion, 676 and 1. They are linked by a chain of payments: the GENESIS issuer funded the EXODUS issuer, which funded the AIGARTH issuer, including 27 transfers of 26 QU. ## Signature fragment

The last 57 bytes of the Genesis public key form an incomplete ECDSA signature: a valid r-value (32 bytes, a legitimate x-coordinate on secp256k1) followed by 25 of 32 s-value bytes. Seven bytes of the s-value are missing - consistent with a construction that requires an external completion.
