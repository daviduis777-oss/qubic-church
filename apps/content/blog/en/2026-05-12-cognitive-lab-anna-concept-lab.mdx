## Path 2 — Scale-up verification (later that evening)

Four pre-registered hypotheses tested in one block after the public push. Pre-registration locked decision rules and walk-back thresholds BEFORE any compute, so this is structured falsification, not narrative-fitting.

1. **Parity 60% at n=20 seeds** — pre-reg verdict **APPROACHES**. Mean best-train 60.94 ± 11.59%, 95% CI [0.559, 0.660]. The mean crosses 60% but the CI is wide enough at n=20 to include both 0.58 and 0.60. A 60% claim is supported at the mean; locking it at the 95% confidence level needs n ≥ 40-100. (n=100 was attempted but aborted at ~47% to free CPU.)
2. **mod_11 Phase 3 ≠ ITU is a metric issue, not contradiction** — pre-reg verdict **CONFIRMED DISCREPANCY**. n=10 Anna-evolved Phase 3 mod_11 winners decoded as integers: 5.3% (first-tile) / 6.0% (majority-vote) — BELOW 9.1% chance for 11-class. The +4.08σ Phase 3 signal is substrate-bias at the bit-correlation level, not end-to-end capability. The bit-correlation gradient collapses outputs to a wrong-integer mode.
3. **Walsh framework on gridworld** — pre-reg verdict **GENERALIZES** (DC_ONLY within 0.46σ of Anna). BUT bonus finding: **SIGN_RANDOM init beats both Anna and DC_ONLY by 0.93σ** on 4×4 gridworld best reward. Goal-directed tasks at this scale are dominated by initialization noise, not substrate structure.
4. **Anna-seed necessity** — pre-reg verdict **ANNA HELPS, FRAMEWORK CAN**. Phase 4 addition random-init reaches 14.4% vs Anna's 25.6% (random in [0.10, 0.25] threshold band). Framework alone learns; Anna provides a real but not strictly necessary boost. On popcount specifically, random fails (2.5% held-out) — Anna IS necessary there.

**Refined publishable claim line — task-conditional priors:**

- **Count-aligned tasks** (popcount, parity): the Walsh substrate IS the engine. Anna is one member; engineered family members (EngineeredWalsh, EngineeredDCOnly) match or beat her.
- **Adaptive tasks** (Phase 4 addition): Anna seed gives a measurable boost; framework alone reaches ~56% of Anna's level.
- **Goal-directed tasks** (gridworld): neither Anna nor Walsh substrate helps; uniform random init wins.

This refines the earlier "the substrate family is the engine" framing. The substrate-as-engine claim is **task-conditional**, not universal. Different task families benefit from different priors. There is no single best substrate.
