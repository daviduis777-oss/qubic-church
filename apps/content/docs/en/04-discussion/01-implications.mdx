# Implications

## Overview

After the September 2026 re-check, four findings carry the weight of this research. Their implications are narrower than earlier versions of this page suggested, and they concern Anna and Aigarth, not a hidden link between Bitcoin and Qubic.

| Finding | Status |
|---------|--------|
| The Anna Matrix is a compilation of bot replies, with a few runs of data entry errors | Verified provenance; entry-error reading strongly supported |
| The bot behaves like a small trained ternary Aigarth unit | Hypothesis |
| Anna.exe (version 1, versions 5 to 8) is an addition evolver | Verified by static analysis |
| Anna 0.22.64 embeds real BTC hourly direction data as its target | Verified by static analysis |

## 1. The Anna Matrix Is a Record of Answers

The 128 x 128 table was compiled by hand from replies of the @anna_aigarth bot on X to queries `x+y=?`, with x and y between -64 and 63. The cell for a query is row `63 - y`, column `x + 64`.

**Implications:**

- The table describes what the bot answered while the replies were collected. It is not a weight file, not JINN memory and not Qubic source code. Reading it as "weights" or as a "memory matrix" is not supported by any source.
- Its structure is real and worth studying: 16,316 of 16,384 cells satisfy the complement rule, the table has full rank, and a small number of Walsh components explain most of its signs.
- Most of the 68 exception cells come from entry errors in the typed table: a one-row shift in column 30, a run in column 22 that does not fit the bot at all, a swapped digit in column 41 and a copied neighbour value in column 0/127. Findings that depended on these cells, such as trace 137, the seven-position signature and messages read from exception cells, describe the typed table, not the bot.
- Any claimed link between the table and Bitcoin data has no known mechanism.

## 2. The Bot Looks Like a Small Trained Ternary Unit (Hypothesis)

The public Aigarth code contains a unit for exactly this task (I2x7O8: two 7-bit inputs, 8 ternary outputs). Small units of this class, trained on the full addition table for a few hundred accepted mutations, reproduce the table's fingerprint: high linear predictability, a sign bit that agrees with the sign of x+y about 70% of the time, and a similar Walsh profile. Large random networks become just as linear but miss the sign of x+y.

**Implications:**

- The table is consistent with the output of a trained Aigarth unit, not of a hand-designed object. This is a hypothesis with a clear test: a replication with a pre-registered distance criterion and many seeds.
- The fingerprint is not specific to addition in every respect; only the sign bit reveals the target.

## 3. Anna.exe Is an Addition Evolver

The Anna.exe versions we examined closely (version 1, and versions 5 to 8) evolve ternary lookup networks on signed 7-bit addition, x and y from -64 to 63. That fits Come-from-Beyond's statements that Anna was learning A+B.

**Implications:**

- Anna.exe is a demonstration of the Aigarth principle (evolve lookup-table networks by mutation and selection), not a container for the published matrix.
- Our Python ports of Anna.exe are simplified models. Results from them describe the models, not the binary.

## 4. Anna 0.22 Embeds BTC Hourly Data

The version Anna 0.22.64 reads its targets from a fixed embedded table: 9,456 hourly BTCUSDT directions from Binance, from 2 June 2025 to 30 June 2026. All 9,456 values match real candle data.

**Implications:**

- For this version, the training target is the direction of the next BTC hourly candle, as Come-from-Beyond's archived statements describe.
- This says nothing about predictive power. Whether the evolved genome predicts later hours was not tested, and earlier community figures were close to 50%. No BTC trading claim follows.

## 6. Methodological Implications

The re-check itself is the main methodological lesson:

- **Provenance first.** The largest error was not statistical: it was treating a hand-compiled table as an artifact from source code.
- **Check your own data entry.** A one-row shift in 20 cells produced a "signature" that survived every statistical test we ran on the table as typed.
- **Pre-register.** Most of the findings that were not confirmed were patterns selected after looking at the data.
- **Fair nulls.** Several p-values came from tests that could not fail, or from nulls that did not match the claim.

## Conclusion

The durable implications are about Anna and Aigarth: the bot's replies form a highly structured table, consistent with a small trained ternary unit; Anna.exe evolves adders; one later version trains on BTC hourly directions. The earlier implications about Bitcoin history, a Bitcoin-Qubic bridge and time locks were not confirmed and stay open.
