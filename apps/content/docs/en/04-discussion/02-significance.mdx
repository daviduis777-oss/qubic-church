# Significance Assessment

## Executive Summary

The findings that survived the September 2026 re-check are modest. They are of interest to people studying Anna and Aigarth, and as a methodological case study. They do not show a link between Bitcoin and Qubic, and they do not revise Bitcoin history.

| Level | Criteria |

## 1. Findings and Their Significance

### 1.1 Provenance of the Anna Matrix

**Significance Level**: High

The table was compiled by hand from @anna_aigarth bot replies to `x+y=?`. This changes how every other matrix finding must be read: the table records answers, not weights or code, and some of its "anomalies" are our own entry errors.

### 1.2 Structure of the Table

**Significance Level**: Medium

The complement rule (16,316 of 16,384 cells), full rank, and a compact Walsh and block structure (26 Walsh coefficients reproduce 81% of the signs) are verified. They describe the bot's answer function and make the table a legitimate object for mathematical study.

### 1.3 The Bot as a Small Trained Unit

**Significance Level**: Medium (hypothesis)

Small public Aigarth units trained on addition reproduce the table's fingerprint. If a pre-registered replication confirms this, it would explain where the structure comes from without any design hypothesis.

### 1.4 Anna.exe and Anna 0.22

**Significance Level**: Medium

Static analysis shows that the Anna.exe versions examined closely evolve adders, and that Anna 0.22.64 trains on real BTC hourly directions. This documents what Come-from-Beyond's public binaries actually do. It does not show predictive power.

### 1.5 Arithmetic Relations With Bitcoin Values

**Significance Level**: Low

Relations such as `1221069728 mod 121 = 43` or the Genesis-1CFB XOR byte sum 2,671 are true as arithmetic. They were found after looking at the data and are not significant.

## 3. Methodological Significance

The most useful result may be the re-check itself. It shows how a research project can accumulate confident claims from a hand-compiled table with a few entry errors, post-hoc pattern selection and nulls that could not fail, and how these can be found and corrected. The practices that caught the errors (going back to raw data, fair null models, pre-registered tests, independent re-computation) are the ones we recommend.

## Conclusion

The significance of this research lies in a careful description of Anna's replies and binaries, and in a documented correction of its own earlier errors. Claims of a Bitcoin-Qubic bridge, a time lock or a revised Bitcoin history are withdrawn.
