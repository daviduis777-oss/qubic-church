# The September 2026 Re-check: what held, what stays open

<Callout type="info" title="In brief">
This page lists what the September 2026 re-check of our research confirmed and what it did not confirm, and points to what we learned along the way. A reading that the re-check did not confirm is an open question, not a closed one. No bridge, node system, "DATE node", time lock or activation mechanism was found between Bitcoin, Qubic and the Anna Matrix.
</Callout>

## Why this page exists

Checking our own work is part of the research. In September 2026 we re-ran the computations, compared them with the raw data and looked for entry errors in the typed table. Several readings were not confirmed, and some of the most striking ones trace back to entry errors in the typed table. Instead of quietly rewriting chapters, we list the results of the re-check here. On 30 September 2026 we also set aside the pages whose main content the re-check did not confirm. Their addresses now lead to this page, and section 7 lists every one of them with what the re-check found.

What we learned in the same re-check is collected in three new chapters: [where the Anna Matrix comes from](/docs/03-results/36-where-the-anna-matrix-comes-from), [Anna 0.22 and Bitcoin price data](/docs/03-results/37-anna-0-22-and-btc) and [CFB and Satoshi: what the primary sources say](/docs/03-results/17-cfb-satoshi-analysis). How the earlier readings came about is described in [How the earlier claims arose](/docs/04-discussion/06-how-the-earlier-claims-arose).

## 1. Where the Anna Matrix comes from

The 128 x 128 table is not a file from Aigarth, from the JINN processor, from Qubic source code or from any Anna binary. Our team compiled it by hand from replies of the @anna_aigarth bot on X, which answered queries of the form `x+y=?` for x and y between -64 and 63. The cell for a query is row `63 - y`, column `x + 64`.

This means:

- Descriptions of the matrix as "pre-trained weights", as the "memory matrix" of the JINN processor, or as something extracted from source code are not supported by any source.
- The table records how the bot answered while we collected the replies. It does not tell us directly how the bot works inside. What we could learn about the program behind the replies is in [chapter 36](/docs/03-results/36-where-the-anna-matrix-comes-from).
- Come-from-Beyond wrote on 14 April 2026: "Anna training is split, part is done by computors. Public statements A+B=C are no longer used." (Discord #aigarth, message 1493553830854393866). On 6 July 2026, in reply to a message that our export does not contain, he wrote: "It will be removed for a while" (message 1523734854603374714).

## 2. The symmetry is real. Some "anomalies" are entry errors in the typed table

- 16,316 of the 16,384 cells satisfy `M[i][j] + M[127-i][127-j] = -1`. In terms of the queries this reads: the answer for the bitwise complements of x and y is the bitwise complement of the answer. For the 51 mirror pairs where the bot answered both cells in our records, the rule held every time.
- The remaining 68 cells (34 pairs) were read by us, and by others, as a hidden layer. About half of them come from one run in column 30 (x = -34, y from 3 down to -16). Shifted by one row, these values match their mirror cells in column 97 almost exactly. A one-row shift explains 94% of that run. Among 2,000 random runs of ordinary cells searched the same way, none reached that level; the best reached 83% (p below 0.001 after correcting for the two runs tested; with 2,000 draws that is the resolution limit). Bot replies confirm the mirror side (x = 33). This is very likely an entry error in the typed table: a block pasted one row off. We call it strongly supported, not proven, because our records contain no bot reply for x = -34.
- A second run sits in column 22 (x = -42, y from 43 down to 31) and its mirror in column 105. A shift does not explain it. It contains the diagonal pair (22,22) and (105,105), both 100, and that pair alone makes the trace of the matrix equal 137. To decide which side fits the bot, we trained a model on the rest of the table only. In calibration runs, where we corrupted one side of an ordinary run on purpose, it picked the corrupted side in 57 to 60 of 60 trials. For this run it finds the column 105 side consistent with the bot (91% of bits) and the column 22 values at chance level (41%). So the 100/100 pair, and with it the trace of 137, very likely come from the typed table rather than from the bot. The corrected, fully symmetric table has a trace of -64. Two smaller cases fit the same picture: at x = -23 the typed table has -101 twice where the bot pattern requires -110, a swapped digit, and in one cell of column 127 a neighbouring value was copied.
- Other statements about the exception cells that were published earlier were not confirmed either. They are not all in the upper half of the table (34 are above, 34 below). The palindrome of the deviations and the column sum of 127 follow from the definition of a mirror pair for any matrix, so they are not additional evidence of design.

## 3. Readings that depend on the column 30 entries

- **Seven-position signature** (page set aside). The period-4 cycles of `sign(M v)` are antipodal except at positions 3, 18, 32, 61, 65, 101 and 109. That is true for the table as typed, and it needs the column 30 values exactly as they were typed. After correcting the column 30 run, the seven positions disappear, for all four resolutions of the other open runs that we tested. If that run is an entry error, which the evidence strongly suggests, the signature describes the typed table rather than the bot. Either way, the link to "seven missing bytes" of the Genesis block signature has no basis in the data.
- **Deviation sum 1161 = 27 x 43.** After the same correction the sum is 1130.
- **Messages read from the exception cells**, including ">FIB" in the XOR of mirror pairs, come from runs that are either corrected or unresolved. They cannot serve as evidence.

## 4. Readings that stay open

- **The Genesis block as a second, executable script** (page set aside). The 65-byte public key in the Genesis block is a valid secp256k1 point. Reading it as Bitcoin Script does not parse, because the data push it would need is incomplete. No hidden second program was found.
- **1CF address enrichment.** Among 21,953 public keys we find 17 addresses that start with `1CF`, and chance predicts 16.5. The earlier "46-fold enrichment" came from Base58 probabilities that do not match the encoding.
- **Bridge "hash collisions" at p below 10^-48.** The matrix cells take at most 256 different values, so different queries often give the same value. For 100 queries about 17 such repeats are expected by chance. Repeats such as 7+7 and 11+11 both giving -20 are not hash collisions, and the p-value below 10^-48 cannot be derived from them.
- **Trace 137 as the fine structure constant.** The value follows from the single 100/100 diagonal pair described above.
- **"Key" at row 8.** The three ASCII values are real, but under a fair null model the pattern is not unusual (185 to 188 hits in 2,000 controls that keep the table's own value distribution; 14 of 2,000 under a cruder model of independent uniform values).
- **"19 concepts" or attractors.** All 16,384 stored end states change on one more step. The 19 are a selection made afterwards, not something the matrix produces.
- **The JINN connection** (page set aside). No source links the table to the JINN processor. Come-from-Beyond called a connection between Aigarth and Jinn "pulled out of nowhere" (15 March 2026, message 1482845747517980883). He was speaking about Aigarth and Jinn in general, not about the table.
- **The March 3, 2026 time lock.** The date comes from the riddle text of the community token GENESIS, which counts 6,268 days from the Bitcoin genesis block, and 3 January 2009 plus 6,268 days is 3 March 2026. On that day a new community token, AIGARTH, was issued with a supply of 1. The date passed without an event: no signal or distribution followed. Earlier versions of this site also cited a CFB message for this date. That message actually reads "No ETA other than April 13, 2027" (message 1394734677935521824). CFB has said that he did not create GENESIS (message 1464222790017224704).
- **Grok path** (page set aside). The way the 40 numbers are mapped to matrix cells, and the address matches reported from that mapping, were chosen after looking at the data and were never tested against a null model. No "master key" was found.

## 6. What held

- The table is highly structured: the complement symmetry, full rank, and a strong, shallow, almost linear component aligned with the sign bits of x and y. It is a legitimate object for mathematical study.
- The Anna.exe versions we examined closely (version 1, and versions 5 to 8) evolve ternary lookup networks on signed 7-bit addition, x and y from -64 to 63. That fits Come-from-Beyond's statements that Anna was learning A+B.
- The public Aigarth code contains a unit with exactly the bot's input and output format. The table behaves like the output of a small trained unit of that kind, but we have not reconstructed it. See [chapter 36](/docs/03-results/36-where-the-anna-matrix-comes-from).

## 8. Further readings that stay open

These statements appeared on pages that remain, or in their earlier versions. The re-check did not confirm them, and they have been removed from those pages.

- **"The Genesis difficulty target has 43 leading zero bits."** The target (nBits 0x1d00ffff) has 32. The block hash that was found has 43.
- **Early block times.** Block 4 was mined on 9 January 2009 at 03:16:28 UTC, block 264 on 13 January 2009 at 00:19:40 UTC and block 576 on 15 January 2009 at 11:51:43 UTC. Earlier versions gave other times.
- **"2671 is divisible by 9."** 2671 mod 9 = 7.
- **"The byte sums of HASH160 values have a standard deviation of 36, so a sum of 2299 is a one-in-millions event."** For 20 random bytes the standard deviation is about 330. Among the roughly 2 million addresses considered, about 7 addresses with first byte 0x7b and byte sum 2299 are expected by chance, and 8 were reported.
- **"2,835 block heights spread over 676 bins with a standard deviation of 0.05."** That is mathematically impossible; the smallest possible standard deviation is 0.395.
- **"Qubic runs 19-tick epochs."** Qubic epochs are seven days.
- **"The byte 0x1b in block 576 is a unique extra field."** It is part of an ordinary ExtraNonce push.
- **"Anna's largest and smallest singular values are 2342 and 0.63."** They are 5435.1 and 1.46.
- **"Anna has a single period-4 cycle, reached in six steps, with neuron 26 passing through zero."** There are at least two cycles, some inputs need 22 steps, and neuron 26 does not pass through zero.
- **"Exactly two cycles for all 3^128 inputs."** This was checked on samples only. It is a hypothesis, not a proof.
- **The "decomposition theorem" and "exactly one irreducible primitive"** in the Anna.exe emergence work are a hypothesis based on selected constructions, not a theorem. A hand-built adder in the Anna.exe substrate used 37 cells in a simplified setting, not "under a third of the cells". 80,000 error-free random tests do not prove correctness for all lengths.
- **"The reimplementation is a faithful port of Anna.exe v1."** It differs from the original in several respects, so its results do not transfer directly to the original.
