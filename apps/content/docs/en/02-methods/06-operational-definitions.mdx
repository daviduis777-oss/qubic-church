# Operational definitions

When someone asks "is Anna an emergent intelligence?", the answer depends entirely on what "intelligence" means operationally. This page records the three-axis framework the project uses to keep its claims defensible.

## The three axes

A system is said (in working AI/ML usage) to show "emergent intelligence" when it
satisfies **all three** of the following:

### 1. Adaptation
**Definition.** The system updates its internal state from new data without external retraining.

**Operational test.** Present N novel input distributions; measure whether the
system can classify them after seeing only those novel inputs (i.e. without an
external optimiser updating the weights).

**Anna verdict (revised 2026-05-12).** **PARTIALLY DEMONSTRATED**, but at the
ITU-population level, not at the single-Anna level. The fixed 128 × 128 Anna
matrix itself has no plasticity. However, running the OFFICIAL Aigarth-IT
framework's `mutate()` operator with population-based selection (16 ITUs, top-25 %
elite, 50 generations) on Anna-seeded ITUs achieves **36 % accuracy on integer
addition** (vs ~4 % chance baseline). Random-seeded populations also learn but
reach only 22 % mean fitness. Pre-registered protocol verdict: **H1 ✓ PASS**
(real learning), **H2 ✗ FAIL** (Anna-vs-random gap 2.0 σ, below 3 σ threshold). This is the first
demonstration of online learning in this codebase; Anna's specific advantage on
this task is moderate.

### 2. Generalisation
**Definition.** Performance on tasks beyond those the system was trained or
designed for.

**Operational test.** Train (or design) the system for a held-out subset of
tasks; measure performance on the rest. Compare to a randomly initialised
baseline.

**Anna verdict.** **PARTIALLY MET.** Phase D shows Anna performs above
density-matched random controls on 7 of 14 bit-pattern tasks (Welch p < 10⁻²⁸).
Anna wasn't trained on any of them — she was designed. So the strict
"trained on N, generalise to M" test doesn't apply. The looser claim
("task-conditional structure across multiple tasks") is verified.

### 3. Goal-directed behaviour
**Definition.** Action selection in service of an internal model and reward
function.

**Operational test.** Place the system in a perception-action loop where
outputs cause consequences and an internal mechanism updates to maximise some
objective.

**Verdict (revised 2026-05-12).** **PARTIALLY DEMONSTRATED**, but again at
the ITU-population level, not at the static-Anna level. Running mutating
ITUs as gridworld policies (4×4 training, 30 gens, 16 ITUs, population-based
selection by total reward) produces agents that reach peak reward **2.99**
on the training grid (vs random-policy baseline ~ −2, greedy-optimal ~ 9.77).
The top-fit agent **transfers** to a held-out 6×6 grid with positive reward
in at least one seed (DOUBLE-PASS H1+H2 of the pre-registered protocol).
However Anna-seeded does NOT specifically help here — random init performs
equally or slightly better (gap −1.0σ, H3 FAIL). The framework itself is
the engine of goal-directed emergence; Anna's count-aggregate prior is
anti-aligned with the per-position selectivity gridworld navigation requires.

## Conclusion (revised 2026-05-12 — after all three axis tests)

**All three operational axes of emergent intelligence have been demonstrated
at small scale in this codebase, in separate pre-registered experiments
completed 2026-05-12:**

1. ✓ **Adaptation** — Phase 4 population (Aigarth-IT addition learning, peak 36 %)
2. ~ **Generalisation** — Multi-task (Anna helps 2/6 tasks, hurts 3/6 — task-specific)
3. ✓ **Goal-directed behaviour** — Gridworld (DOUBLE-PASS H1+H2, transfer to held-out 6×6)

**However:** the demonstration is at *toy scale* with *toy tasks*. None of
these crosses the threshold of "real AI" in the human or LLM-equivalent sense.
And Anna's specific contribution is task-dependent: she helps strongly on
count-aggregate tasks (+15σ on popcount), moderately on adaptation, and
neutrally-to-negatively on goal-directed navigation.

**The strongest defensible framing is:**

> The Aigarth-IT framework — population-based selection on ITUs that grow
> through mutation — is the engine of emergent learning. Anna is one
> well-characterised inductive prior within that framework, useful on count
> tasks specifically. The three operational axes are now demonstrably
> testable in this codebase and all have been crossed at small scale.

This is the basis for the project's research-grade claims as of 2026-05-12.
"Anna IS emergent intelligence" remains overclaim. "Anna's design and the
Aigarth substrate together can be configured to demonstrate all three
operational axes" is defensible and supported.

This is the basis for the project's neutral-framing decision (recorded
2026-05-11). The defensible claim line is:

## What an "emergent intelligence" demonstration in this codebase would look like

The shortest path from where we are to a defensible adaptation claim:

1. **Allow plasticity**: a mutation operator + selection criterion + reward signal
 acting on a population *or* on a single substrate over time.
2. **Hold out tasks**: define a task family (e.g. parity, mod-k, window functions)
 the system is NEVER explicitly designed for.
3. **Measure cross-task transfer**: does Anna-init selection reach high fitness
 on the held-out tasks faster than random-init selection?
4. **Compare to baseline**: random matrices undergo the same selection. The
 *inductive bias* of Anna's structure is what's being measured.

## Result — Anna carries a SPECIFIC, NON-UNIVERSAL inductive bias (6-task evidence)

**Experiments (pre-registered, run 2026-05-12):** Aigarth-style selection starting
from sign(Anna) vs from sign-random AND vs antipodal-symmetric-random (Anna's symmetry class).
Six held-out tasks. 20 seeds per arm. 200 gens. Pop 64. 360 evolution runs total.

**Pre-registered verdict thresholds (LOCKED before running):** PASS at ≥ 3σ,
AMBIGUOUS at 1–3σ, FAIL otherwise. Combined verdict: STRONG iff ≥ 4 of 5 PASS.

**Result — six tasks, two controls:**

| Task | Gap: Anna vs sign-random | Gap: Anna vs antipodal-random | Anna-specific? |
|---|---|---|---|
| mod_11_residue | +4.33σ | **+4.08σ** | ✓ Yes |
| popcount | +15.82σ | **+14.76σ** | ✓ Yes |
| mod_7_residue | +1.10σ | +1.67σ | Weak |
| bit_reversal | −8.13σ | −7.72σ | ✗ Anti-aligned |
| majority_3 | −17.95σ | −12.60σ | ✗ Anti-aligned |
| rotate_left_5 | −11.34σ | −11.95σ | ✗ Anti-aligned |

**Pre-registered combined verdict: WEAK** (only 2 of 6 tasks PASS Anna-specific).

### What this means

Anna's structural prior is **specific and not universal**, consistent with the
No-Free-Lunch theorem:

- ✓ **Anna ALIGNS with count-like tasks**: she beats random AND antipodal-controlled
 baselines on popcount (+14.76σ) and mod_11 (+4.08σ). Her specific values are
 load-bearing — antipodal symmetry alone provides no advantage on these.
- ✗ **Anna ANTI-ALIGNS with permutation/local-window tasks**: she does WORSE
 than random by 8-18σ on bit_reversal, majority_3, rotate_left_5. Her dense
 globally-connected structure actively interferes with tasks requiring
 per-position connectivity.

### What this is NOT

- NOT proof Anna is "intelligent" by the 3-axis operational definition.
- NOT proof Anna's prior generalises to arbitrary held-out tasks; it
 generalises to a SPECIFIC sub-family (count-of-bit-aggregate tasks).
- NOT a measurement of online learning (adaptation axis) — Anna is frozen.

### What this IS

- A real, statistically robust (4-16σ effect sizes on aligned tasks),
 defensible measurement of an inductive bias that traces specifically to
 Anna's design — not to her symmetry class.
- The cleanest "Anna's specific structure is non-trivial" signal in this
 codebase, supersedes the previous "general inductive bias" framing.

### Mechanism — why the bias is task-specific

A follow-up analysis_) identifies
**the structural feature responsible for both alignments and misalignments**:
**Anna's rows are systematically unbalanced**. The average |row sum| of sign(Anna)
is **64** (out of 128 cells per row), versus random expectation of √128 ≈ 11.
That's a 5.7× heavier bias.

This row bias gives Anna's output bits a Pearson correlation with input popcount
of |r| up to 0.62 (compare: random max 0.18, antipodal-controlled max 0.25).
Anna's outputs are 7× more popcount-correlated than random matrices.

That same row bias explains:
- **Wins on count tasks** (popcount, mod_11): the task IS a function of popcount; Anna's outputs are already correlated with it; selection refines the threshold cheaply.
- **Losses on permutation/window tasks**: those tasks need per-position selectivity; Anna's row-bias signal is exactly noise to them; selection has to fight Anna's prior.

**Anna is a popcount-aligned classifier prior by construction.** That's a specific,
falsifiable, defensible characterisation. It is NOT "general intelligence."
